Once you're comfortable with the basics, there's a whole layer of efficiency that separates "I use Salesforce" from "Salesforce works for me." This module is a grab bag of the tricks that make daily life faster.

![Power user cheat sheet: inline editing (double-click fields in list views), pinned views (set your default view per object), favorites (star records and reports for quick access), and keyboard shortcuts (/ for search, Cmd+S to save).]({{ '/assets/images/power-user-tips.svg' | relative_url }})

### Inline Editing from List Views

You don't have to open every record to edit it. In most list views, you can double-click a field directly in the table to edit it. Need to update the status on ten records? Scroll through the list, double-click each status field, change it, and tab to the next one. No page loads, no waiting.

### Pinned List Views

When you open an object's tab, Salesforce shows your most recently used list view. You can **pin** a list view to make it the default every time you navigate to that object. If you built a great filtered view of your key accounts, pin it so it's always the first thing you see.

### Favorites

The star icon in your browser's navigation bar lets you favorite specific records, list views, reports, and dashboards. Your favorites appear in a quick-access menu. If there are five records or reports you check every week, favorite them and save yourself the search.

### Keyboard Shortcuts

Salesforce supports keyboard shortcuts for common actions. Press `/` to jump to Global Search. Use `Ctrl+S` (or `Cmd+S`) to save records. These sound small, but they add up across hundreds of daily interactions.

### The URL Bar Is Your Friend

Salesforce URLs follow predictable patterns. The record ID (the 15 or 18-character string) appears in the URL. You can bookmark specific records, share URLs with colleagues via Slack, or even construct URLs that open new record creation forms with pre-filled fields. This is an advanced trick, but once you see it, you'll use it.

### The Mobile App

The Salesforce mobile app is genuinely useful for specific scenarios: checking a record before walking into a meeting, logging a quick note after a phone call, looking up a contact's information when you're at a conference. It's less useful for complex data entry or report building. Know what it's good for and use it for those things.

### Account Hierarchy Navigation

At OpenStax, institutions sometimes exist in hierarchies: a school district is the parent Account, and individual schools are children underneath it. When you're on a school's Account record, you can navigate up to its parent district (and see all sibling schools) by clicking the parent Account in the hierarchy field. This is particularly useful for understanding the K-12 landscape.
