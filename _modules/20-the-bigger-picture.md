Let's follow one small event all the way through, to see where a single record ends up.

Dr. Maya Rivera teaches Anatomy & Physiology at Cedar Bluff Community College. She has used *Anatomy and Physiology 2e* for years. In the fall of 2026 she gets OpenStax's annual renewal email, clicks the link, and confirms she's using the book again in 2026 - 27. It takes her about thirty seconds.

## What happens next

1. **A new Adoption is created.** It's an **OpenStax Confirmed Adoption** for school year 2026 - 27, with her student count and the estimated savings.
2. **Her Book Opp returns to "Active - Book Currently in Use."** Automation moves the stage because there's now a confirmed adoption this year. Nobody drags anything.
3. **Her Contact updates.** Adoption Status reads "Current Adopter." Her Engagement Tier stays Champion (adopter, three or more years, recently engaged).
4. **Cedar Bluff's counts refresh overnight.** The school's adoption counts go up by one, and its Assignable Fit Tier is recalculated from how many instructors there have confirmed adoptions.
5. **Marketing segments update.** Her next message is a thank-you, not "Have you considered adopting?"
6. **openstax.org shows her renewal as done**, so her 2026 - 27 confirmation is visible to her on the site.
7. **Tableau's impact numbers include her students the next day**, after the daily refresh. They count toward students reached and estimated savings.
8. **The next CX agent sees all of it.** If Dr. Rivera opens a Case next week, the agent sees a long-time Champion at an active school, and can answer accordingly.

![One renewal click leads to eight downstream effects: a new Adoption, Book Opp back to Active, Contact status, school counts, marketing, openstax.org, Tableau, and support.]({{ '/assets/images/ripple-cascade.svg' | relative_url }})

Nobody in that chain did anything by hand. That's the point of [Machines and People]({{ '/modules/04-machines-and-people/' | relative_url }}): the system handles volume and speed. If the renewal had come instead from a conversation with her department chair, a person would have logged it with the Build Adoption flow, and the same chain would have run from step 1. For how the pieces fit, see [Adoptions]({{ '/modules/12-adoptions-and-impact/' | relative_url }}) and [Reading a Contact]({{ '/modules/11-reading-a-contact/' | relative_url }}).

## What people add

Automation can't know most of what's worth knowing. A CX agent tags a Case correctly, so the support trends mean something. Someone notices an email bounced and fixes the address. A rep logs a call with an outcome and a note about what the instructor said. Someone picks the right school for a Lead the machine couldn't match. Someone merges a duplicate before it splits one instructor into two.

Neither side is enough alone. The machines are fast and consistent; people notice what's off and add the "why."

**Try it:** Next time you update a record, ask who will read it next: a teammate on a Case, a marketer building a segment, or an analyst checking a total. Then make your note useful to them.

## Why it matters here

OpenStax exists to make education more accessible. The records in Salesforce are how the organization knows who it's reaching: which schools, which books, how many students, where support is needed. When those records are accurate, OpenStax can tell its story honestly and decide where to invest next. When they drift, the story gets blurry.

Which brings us to the self-aware part: a nonprofit that gives textbooks away has written a free book about managing the data behind them. We noticed. We wrote it anyway, because the people who use this system are the ones who make it work.
