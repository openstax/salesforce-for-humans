OpenStax has relationships with hundreds of thousands of educators across thousands of institutions. Faculty members adopt free textbooks. Students purchase access to the Assignable homework platform. Donors fund the mission. Partners build courseware integrations. Support agents field questions across email, chat, and phone. Marketing reaches educators through targeted campaigns and webinars.

Every one of those interactions generates knowledge. A support agent learns that an instructor at Houston Community College is having trouble with their LMS integration. A sales rep discovers that a department chair at a community college in Arizona is evaluating homework platforms for fall. A marketing team member notices that webinar attendees from two-year colleges convert to adoptions at a higher rate.

The problem with knowledge is that it's fragile. It lives in people's heads, their inboxes, their notebooks, and their memory of conversations at conferences. When someone leaves the team, changes roles, or just has a busy week, that knowledge goes dark. The instructor who mentioned their LMS problem? If that wasn't written down somewhere everyone can see it, the sales rep who follows up three weeks later walks into the conversation blind. The support agent who picks up the next call has no idea what's already been discussed.

**Customer Relationship Management** (CRM) exists to solve this problem. It's a shared memory system, a place where every interaction, every adoption, every support case, every campaign touchpoint, and every pipeline movement is recorded and connected. When it works well, anyone at OpenStax can open a record and see the full picture of a relationship without having to ask five people in Slack.

Salesforce is that shared memory for OpenStax. It's where we track:

- **Who our educators are** and which institutions they belong to
- **Which textbooks are being adopted**, by whom, and how many students they reach
- **Every support interaction**, from the initial question to the resolution
- **Marketing engagement**, including which emails were opened, which webinars were attended, and which campaigns led to adoptions
- **The Assignable pipeline**, from instructor interest through student payment
- **Mission impact**, including the students reached and textbook savings generated every school year

None of this works if only one team participates. The value of a CRM scales directly with how many people contribute to it. A record that only marketing updates is useful to marketing. A record that marketing, support, sales, and the data team all touch becomes a complete portrait of a relationship — and that portrait is what powers better support calls, smarter outreach, more personalized web experiences, and more accurate mission reporting.

That's what this book is about. Not the technical details of how Salesforce is configured, but the living system underneath it: how OpenStax organizes its knowledge, how that knowledge flows between teams, and how your individual contributions to the system compound into organizational intelligence.
